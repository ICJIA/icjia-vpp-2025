# Illinois Awards $2.3 Million in Community Violence Prevention Grants

The Illinois Criminal Justice Information Authority (ICJIA) today announced the award of $2.3 million in federal funding to support community-based violence prevention programs across 15 counties. This funding represents a significant investment in evidence-based interventions designed to reduce violence and support communities most impacted by trauma.

## Grant Recipients and Programs

The funding will support a diverse range of programs, including:

- **Hospital-based violence intervention programs** in Chicago, Rockford, and Peoria
- **Community outreach and engagement initiatives** in East St. Louis and Aurora
- **Youth mentorship and job training programs** in Springfield and Decatur
- **Trauma-informed mental health services** in Champaign-Urbana and Joliet

## Evidence-Based Approaches

All funded programs must demonstrate adherence to evidence-based practices, including:

1. **Cure Violence model** implementation in high-risk neighborhoods
2. **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy** for individuals at risk of violence involvement
3. **Wraparound services** connecting participants to housing, education, and employment
4. **Family-based interventions** addressing intergenerational trauma

## Expected Outcomes

The programs are expected to serve approximately 1,200 individuals over the next 18 months, with measurable outcomes including:

- 25% reduction in violent incidents in target areas
- 80% of participants completing program requirements
- 60% of participants securing stable employment or education
- Improved community trust and engagement with violence prevention efforts

## Community Partnership Model

"These grants represent our commitment to supporting community-led solutions to violence," said Dr. Sarah Johnson, Executive Director of ICJIA. "By investing in local organizations that understand their communities' unique needs, we can create lasting change that goes beyond traditional law enforcement approaches."

The funding emphasizes collaboration between:
- Community-based organizations
- Healthcare systems
- Educational institutions
- Faith-based organizations
- Local government agencies

## Application and Implementation

Organizations were selected through a competitive application process that prioritized:
- Demonstrated community need and support
- Evidence-based program design
- Organizational capacity and experience
- Sustainability planning beyond the grant period

Programs will begin implementation in February 2025, with ongoing technical assistance and evaluation support provided by the University of Illinois Violence Prevention Research Lab.

For more information about the Violence Prevention Plan for Illinois and funding opportunities, visit our [strategic priorities](/strategic-priorities) page or contact the ICJIA Violence Prevention team.
