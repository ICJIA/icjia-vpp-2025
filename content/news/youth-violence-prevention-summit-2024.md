# Statewide Youth Violence Prevention Summit Brings Together 300+ Advocates

More than 300 violence prevention advocates, researchers, and community leaders gathered in Springfield for the third annual Illinois Youth Violence Prevention Summit, hosted by the Illinois Criminal Justice Information Authority and the University of Illinois Violence Prevention Research Lab.

## Key Summit Highlights

The two-day summit featured presentations on cutting-edge research, innovative program models, and collaborative strategies for addressing youth violence in both urban and rural communities across Illinois.

### Research Presentations

Dr. Maria Rodriguez from the University of Illinois presented new findings on the effectiveness of school-based violence prevention programs, showing:

- **40% reduction** in disciplinary incidents in schools implementing comprehensive programs
- **Improved academic outcomes** for students participating in conflict resolution training
- **Enhanced school climate** as measured by student and teacher surveys

### Innovative Program Models

Several Illinois communities showcased successful violence prevention initiatives:

#### Chicago's "Becoming a Man" Program
- Serves 1,500+ young men annually
- Combines cognitive behavioral therapy with mentorship
- Demonstrated 50% reduction in violent crime arrests among participants

#### Rockford's Community Healing Initiative
- Addresses trauma in neighborhoods affected by violence
- Integrates mental health services with community organizing
- Engages 200+ families in healing-centered activities

#### Rural Violence Prevention Network
- Connects small communities across central Illinois
- Shares resources and best practices
- Addresses unique challenges in rural areas

## Policy Recommendations

Summit participants developed key policy recommendations for the 2025 legislative session:

1. **Increased funding** for evidence-based violence prevention programs
2. **Enhanced coordination** between state agencies and community organizations
3. **Improved data collection** to track violence prevention outcomes
4. **Expanded mental health services** in schools and communities

## Youth Leadership

A highlight of the summit was the Youth Leadership Panel, featuring young people from across Illinois who shared their experiences and recommendations:

> "We need programs that see us as leaders, not just problems to be solved. When adults invest in our potential, we can transform our communities." - Jasmine Williams, Chicago

> "In rural areas, we face different challenges but the same need for hope and opportunity. Violence prevention has to look different in small towns." - Marcus Thompson, Carbondale

## Next Steps

The summit concluded with commitments to:

- Establish regional violence prevention networks
- Develop shared evaluation metrics across programs
- Create a statewide youth advisory council
- Plan the 2025 summit with expanded rural participation

## Resources and Support

Participants received access to:
- Toolkit for implementing evidence-based programs
- Funding opportunity database
- Technical assistance network
- Online community platform for ongoing collaboration
