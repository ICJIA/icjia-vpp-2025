# Hospital Violence Intervention Programs Expand to Five New Illinois Cities

Five Illinois hospitals are launching new violence intervention programs designed to break the cycle of violence by providing support and resources to patients treated for violent injuries. The expansion builds on successful models already operating in Chicago and East St. Louis.

## Program Overview

Hospital Violence Intervention Programs (HVIPs) work by:

- **Identifying patients** at high risk for repeat violence exposure
- **Providing immediate crisis intervention** during the vulnerable hospital stay
- **Connecting patients to community resources** including housing, employment, and mental health services
- **Following up with participants** for 6-12 months after discharge

## New Program Locations

### Peoria - OSF Saint Francis Medical Center
- Serving the greater Peoria metropolitan area
- Focus on youth ages 16-24
- Partnership with Peoria County Health Department

### Rockford - SwedishAmerican Hospital
- Addressing violence in Winnebago County
- Bilingual services in English and Spanish
- Collaboration with Rockford Public Schools

### Aurora - Rush Copley Medical Center
- Serving Kane County communities
- Emphasis on family-based interventions
- Integration with existing mental health services

### Joliet - AMITA Health Saint Joseph Medical Center
- Covering Will County region
- Specialized trauma-informed care approach
- Partnership with local faith-based organizations

### Champaign - Carle Foundation Hospital
- Serving Champaign-Urbana area
- University of Illinois research collaboration
- Focus on evidence-based evaluation

## Evidence Base

Research from existing Illinois HVIPs demonstrates significant impact:

- **58% reduction** in repeat violent injury among participants
- **45% decrease** in emergency department visits for violence-related injuries
- **70% of participants** successfully connected to community services
- **$3.2 million** in estimated healthcare cost savings annually

## Training and Implementation

All new programs will receive comprehensive training and technical assistance:

### Staff Training Components
- Trauma-informed care principles
- Crisis intervention techniques
- Community resource navigation
- Cultural competency and bias awareness

### Implementation Support
- 12-month technical assistance period
- Monthly coaching calls with experienced programs
- Shared evaluation tools and data systems
- Quarterly learning collaborative meetings

## Community Partnerships

Each program emphasizes strong community partnerships:

- **Community-based organizations** providing wraparound services
- **Educational institutions** offering GED and job training programs
- **Faith-based organizations** providing spiritual and social support
- **Employers** committed to hiring program participants

## Funding and Sustainability

The expansion is supported by:
- Federal Victims of Crime Act funding
- State violence prevention appropriations
- Hospital foundation grants
- Local community foundation support

Programs are developing sustainability plans including:
- Integration with hospital operations
- Medicaid reimbursement strategies
- Corporate sponsorship opportunities
- Fee-for-service training programs

## Expected Outcomes

Over the next three years, the expanded programs expect to:

- Serve 800+ individuals annually across all sites
- Reduce repeat violent injuries by 50%
- Connect 75% of participants to stable housing
- Help 60% of participants secure employment or education
- Save an estimated $5 million in healthcare costs

## Research and Evaluation

The University of Illinois Violence Prevention Research Lab will conduct comprehensive evaluation of all programs, examining:

- Individual participant outcomes
- Community-level violence reduction
- Cost-effectiveness analysis
- Implementation best practices
