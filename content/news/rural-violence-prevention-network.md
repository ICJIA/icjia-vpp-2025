# New Rural Violence Prevention Network Addresses Unique Challenges in Small Communities

The Illinois Criminal Justice Information Authority today announced the launch of the Illinois Rural Violence Prevention Network, a first-of-its-kind initiative designed to address the unique challenges of preventing violence in rural and small-town communities across the state.

## Understanding Rural Violence

Violence in rural Illinois presents distinct challenges that differ significantly from urban areas:

### Unique Risk Factors
- **Geographic isolation** limiting access to services
- **Economic challenges** including job loss and poverty
- **Limited mental health resources** and stigma around seeking help
- **Higher rates of firearm ownership** and suicide risk
- **Substance abuse** particularly opioid and methamphetamine use

### Service Delivery Challenges
- **Transportation barriers** to accessing services
- **Provider shortages** in mental health and social services
- **Lack of anonymity** in small communities affecting help-seeking
- **Limited funding** for rural-specific programs
- **Technology gaps** affecting telehealth and online resources

## Network Structure and Membership

The network connects 28 rural counties across Illinois, organized into five regional hubs:

### Northern Illinois Hub
- **Counties**: Jo Daviess, Stephenson, Carroll, Whiteside, Lee
- **Lead Organization**: Northwestern Illinois Community Action Agency
- **Focus**: Agricultural community violence prevention

### Central Illinois Hub
- **Counties**: Mason, Menard, Logan, DeWitt, Piatt, Moultrie
- **Lead Organization**: Central Illinois Rural Health Network
- **Focus**: Suicide prevention and mental health

### Southern Illinois Hub
- **Counties**: Franklin, Williamson, Saline, Gallatin, Hardin
- **Lead Organization**: Southern Illinois Healthcare Foundation
- **Focus**: Domestic violence and substance abuse

### Western Illinois Hub
- **Counties**: Henderson, Warren, McDonough, Schuyler, Brown
- **Lead Organization**: Western Illinois Regional Council
- **Focus**: Youth violence and school-based prevention

### Eastern Illinois Hub
- **Counties**: Vermilion, Edgar, Clark, Coles, Cumberland
- **Lead Organization**: Eastern Illinois Area Agency on Aging
- **Focus**: Elder abuse and family violence

## Evidence-Based Rural Adaptations

The network is implementing evidence-based programs adapted for rural contexts:

### Telehealth Violence Intervention
- **Remote counseling** for violence survivors and perpetrators
- **Mobile crisis response** teams serving multiple counties
- **Digital support groups** connecting isolated individuals
- **Online training** for local service providers

### Community-Based Approaches
- **Faith-based partnerships** leveraging existing trust networks
- **Agricultural extension integration** reaching farming families
- **School-based programs** adapted for small rural schools
- **Volunteer coordinator models** maximizing limited resources

### Mobile Service Delivery
- **Traveling counselors** serving multiple communities
- **Mobile crisis units** providing immediate response
- **Outreach vans** bringing services to remote areas
- **Pop-up resource fairs** in community gathering spaces

## Innovative Program Models

### Neighbor-to-Neighbor Support Network
- Trains community members to recognize warning signs
- Provides safe spaces for disclosure and support
- Connects individuals to professional resources
- Maintains confidentiality in small communities

### Rural Youth Leadership Program
- Engages young people as violence prevention advocates
- Provides leadership development and job skills
- Creates positive peer networks across counties
- Addresses rural youth exodus and hopelessness

### Agricultural Stress and Violence Prevention
- Addresses unique stressors facing farming families
- Provides financial counseling and crisis support
- Trains agricultural professionals to recognize warning signs
- Connects families to mental health and violence prevention resources

## Technology and Innovation

The network leverages technology to overcome geographic barriers:

### Digital Platform Features
- **Secure messaging** between providers and clients
- **Resource mapping** showing available services by location
- **Training modules** for community volunteers
- **Data collection** tools for program evaluation

### Mobile Applications
- **Crisis hotline** with GPS location services
- **Safety planning** tools for domestic violence survivors
- **Mental health screening** and resource connection
- **Anonymous reporting** for community violence concerns

## Funding and Sustainability

The network is supported through:

- **Federal rural health grants** totaling $1.8 million
- **State violence prevention funding** allocated specifically for rural areas
- **Private foundation support** from agricultural and rural-focused foundations
- **Local matching funds** from participating counties and organizations

### Sustainability Strategies
- **Fee-for-service training** programs for other states
- **Medicaid reimbursement** for telehealth services
- **Corporate partnerships** with agricultural businesses
- **Volunteer coordination** to maximize limited resources

## Measuring Impact

The network will track outcomes specific to rural contexts:

### Individual Outcomes
- Reduction in domestic violence incidents
- Decreased suicide rates and attempts
- Improved access to mental health services
- Increased help-seeking behavior

### Community Outcomes
- Enhanced community capacity for violence prevention
- Improved coordination between service providers
- Increased awareness of violence prevention resources
- Strengthened social connections and support networks

## Early Results

Since the pilot launch in six counties:

- **150+ individuals** have received direct services
- **45 community volunteers** have been trained
- **12 faith-based organizations** have joined as partners
- **3 mobile crisis responses** have been successfully deployed

## Getting Involved

Rural communities interested in joining the network can:

- **Contact their regional hub** coordinator
- **Attend monthly virtual meetings** for network members
- **Apply for mini-grants** to support local violence prevention efforts
- **Participate in training** opportunities and resource sharing

## National Model

The Illinois Rural Violence Prevention Network is being watched by other states as a potential model for rural violence prevention. The network is:

- **Documenting best practices** for replication
- **Sharing evaluation results** with national partners
- **Presenting at conferences** on rural violence prevention
- **Collaborating with researchers** on rural-specific studies

This initiative represents Illinois' commitment to ensuring that all communities, regardless of size or location, have access to effective violence prevention resources. Learn more about our [statewide approach](/strategic-priorities) and [community partnerships](/about).
