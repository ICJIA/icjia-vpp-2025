## Our Mission: Creating Safer Communities Across Illinois

::text-wrap-image{src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1521737604893-d14cc237f11d?w=200&h=200&fit=crop&crop=center" alt="Business team working together placeholder image demonstrating text wrapping functionality" width="200" height="200" align="left" spacing="medium" caption="Sample Image"}

The Statewide Violence Prevention Plan for Illinois: 2025-2029 represents a comprehensive, collaborative effort to address violence as a preventable public health crisis. Our mission is to create safer, healthier communities across Illinois through evidence-based prevention strategies, community partnerships, and trauma-informed approaches.

Violence affects individuals, families, and communities in profound ways, creating ripple effects that extend far beyond the immediate incident. We recognize that preventing violence requires a coordinated response that addresses root causes, builds protective factors, and strengthens community resilience. This plan serves as a roadmap for state agencies, local organizations, community leaders, and residents to work together toward a common goal: reducing violence and promoting healing in our communities.

Our approach is grounded in the understanding that violence prevention is not just the responsibility of law enforcement or social services—it requires all of us. From educators and healthcare providers to faith leaders and business owners, everyone has a role to play in creating the conditions where violence is less likely to occur and where healing can take place when it does.
::

## Collaborative Approach to Violence Prevention

The development of this plan involved extensive collaboration with stakeholders across Illinois, including state agencies, local governments, community-based organizations, advocacy groups, survivors, and residents from communities most impacted by violence. This collaborative approach ensures that our strategies are informed by diverse perspectives and grounded in the lived experiences of those we aim to serve.

::text-wrap-image{src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1600880292203-757bb62b4baf?w=180&h=180&fit=crop&crop=center" alt="Diverse group discussion placeholder image demonstrating right-aligned text wrapping" width="180" height="180" align="right" spacing="large" caption="Placeholder Image"}

We believe that sustainable violence prevention requires authentic community engagement and shared ownership of solutions. Our planning process prioritized the voices of those with lived experience of violence, recognizing that they are the true experts on what works in their communities. This includes survivors of violence, family members, community leaders, and residents who have witnessed the impacts of violence firsthand.

The plan also reflects input from professionals across multiple sectors, including public health, criminal justice, education, healthcare, social services, and economic development. This cross-sector collaboration is essential because violence is a complex issue that requires coordinated responses across multiple systems and institutions.

Our commitment to collaboration extends beyond the planning phase. Implementation of this plan will require ongoing partnership and coordination among all stakeholders. We are committed to maintaining open lines of communication, sharing resources and expertise, and working together to adapt our strategies based on what we learn along the way.
::

### Core Principles Guiding Our Work

Our violence prevention efforts are guided by five core principles that ensure our work is grounded in equity, safety, health, belonging, and collaboration:

* **Foster Belonging and Social Connectedness**: Creating opportunities for individuals and communities to develop healthy, peaceful relationships and find acceptance
* **Promote Equity and Address Systemic Oppression**: Recognizing and addressing the structural inequities that contribute to violence and its disproportionate impacts
* **Prioritize Safety and Healing**: Ensuring that our prevention efforts do not cause additional harm and actively promote healing for those affected by violence
* **Strengthen Community Capacity**: Building the skills, resources, and infrastructure needed for communities to prevent violence and respond effectively when it occurs
* **Use Data and Evidence**: Making decisions based on the best available research and data while remaining responsive to community needs and feedback

## Implementation Strategy and Community Engagement

::text-wrap-image{src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1556761175-b413da4baf72?w=220&h=220&fit=crop&crop=center" alt="Community outreach and engagement placeholder image demonstrating left-aligned text wrapping with larger dimensions" width="220" height="220" align="left" spacing="medium" caption="Demo Image"}

The success of this plan depends on effective implementation at the state, regional, and local levels. Our implementation strategy emphasizes flexibility and adaptability, recognizing that different communities face different challenges and have different strengths and resources.

We are committed to providing technical assistance, training, and resources to support local implementation efforts. This includes developing toolkits and guidance documents, facilitating peer learning opportunities, and connecting communities with funding and other resources. We also recognize the importance of building local capacity for violence prevention, including training community members to become violence prevention advocates and leaders.

Monitoring and evaluation are integral components of our implementation strategy. We will track progress toward our goals using both quantitative indicators (such as rates of violence) and qualitative measures (such as community perceptions of safety and well-being). This data will help us understand what is working, what needs to be adjusted, and where additional resources or support may be needed.

Community engagement remains central to our implementation approach. We will continue to seek input from communities throughout the implementation process, ensuring that our strategies remain relevant and responsive to changing needs and circumstances. This includes regular community forums, surveys, and other opportunities for feedback and dialogue.
::

## Expected Outcomes and Long-Term Impact

Through the implementation of this comprehensive violence prevention plan, we anticipate significant positive changes across Illinois communities over the next five years. Our expected outcomes are organized around three key areas: reducing violence, strengthening communities, and improving systems responses.

**Reducing Violence**: We expect to see measurable decreases in various forms of violence, including intimate partner violence, child abuse and neglect, sexual violence, and community violence. These reductions will be achieved through primary prevention strategies that address root causes, secondary prevention efforts that intervene with at-risk populations, and tertiary prevention services that support healing and prevent re-victimization.

**Strengthening Communities**: We anticipate increased community capacity for violence prevention, including stronger social connections, improved economic opportunities, enhanced community leadership, and greater access to resources and services. Communities will be better equipped to identify and address risk factors for violence while building protective factors that promote safety and well-being.

**Improving Systems Responses**: We expect to see more coordinated, trauma-informed, and culturally responsive services across sectors. This includes improved training for professionals, better coordination between agencies, enhanced data collection and sharing, and more effective policies and practices that support violence prevention and survivor services.

### Measuring Success and Continuous Improvement

Success will be measured through a comprehensive evaluation framework that includes both quantitative and qualitative indicators. We will track changes in violence rates, community perceptions of safety, access to services, and other key metrics. Equally important, we will gather feedback from communities about their experiences and perspectives on the effectiveness of our strategies.

* **Data-Driven Decision Making**: Regular analysis of trends and patterns to inform strategy adjustments
* **Community Feedback Integration**: Ongoing input from residents and stakeholders to ensure relevance and effectiveness
* **Evidence-Based Practice Adoption**: Continuous incorporation of new research and best practices

### Getting Involved and Staying Connected

Violence prevention is most effective when it involves the entire community. There are many ways for individuals, organizations, and communities to get involved in this important work. Whether you are a survivor, family member, community leader, service provider, or concerned resident, your voice and participation matter.

**For Individuals**: You can get involved by learning about violence prevention, supporting survivors in your community, advocating for policy changes, volunteering with local organizations, and modeling healthy relationships and conflict resolution in your own life.

**For Organizations**: Community-based organizations, faith communities, schools, businesses, and other institutions can integrate violence prevention into their programming, policies, and practices. This might include implementing prevention education, creating safer environments, supporting employees or members affected by violence, and partnering with other organizations on prevention initiatives.

**For Communities**: Local governments, coalitions, and community groups can adopt comprehensive violence prevention strategies, advocate for resources and policy changes, engage residents in prevention efforts, and work to address the underlying conditions that contribute to violence.
